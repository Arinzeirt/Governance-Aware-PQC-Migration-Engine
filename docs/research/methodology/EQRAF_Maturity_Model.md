# EQRAF Enterprise Maturity Model

Version: 1.0 (Research Draft)

Framework: Enterprise Quantum Readiness Assessment Framework (EQRAF)

Platform: Enterprise Quantum Migration Platform (EQMP)

---

# Purpose

The EQRAF Enterprise Maturity Model provides a structured methodology for evaluating an organisation's readiness for enterprise-wide post-quantum transformation.

Rather than producing a simple numerical score, the model measures organisational capability across multiple assessment domains and assigns an enterprise maturity level.

The maturity model enables organisations to understand their current capability, identify improvement opportunities and measure progress over time.

---

# Design Principles

The maturity model is based on five principles.

• Governance before technology.

• Enterprise-wide assessment rather than technical assessment.

• Risk-driven decision making.

• Executive-friendly reporting.

• Continuous organisational improvement.

---

# Assessment Domains

The maturity model evaluates five enterprise domains.

## Domain 1

Enterprise Profile

Purpose

Provides organisational context for the assessment.

Example indicators

- Industry
- Organisation size
- Geographic footprint
- Critical infrastructure classification

---

## Domain 2

Technology Landscape

Purpose

Measures enterprise technology complexity.

Example indicators

- Cloud adoption
- Identity infrastructure
- PKI deployment
- HSM usage
- Enterprise applications
- API ecosystem

---

## Domain 3

Cryptographic Posture

Purpose

Measures cryptographic exposure and migration complexity.

Example indicators

- Algorithms in use
- Certificate inventory
- Cryptographic technologies
- Long-term sensitive data
- Cryptographic agility

---

## Domain 4

Governance & Risk

Purpose

Measures organisational capability to manage transformation.

Example indicators

- Executive sponsorship
- Governance committee
- Risk management
- Compliance programme
- Migration strategy

---

## Domain 5

Transformation Readiness

Purpose

Transformation Readiness is calculated from the previous four domains.

It represents the organisation's overall capability to successfully execute enterprise-wide post-quantum transformation.

---

# Enterprise Maturity Levels

EQRAF defines five maturity levels.

## Level 1 — Emerging

Characteristics

- Limited awareness
- No formal governance
- Minimal cryptographic visibility
- Reactive security practices

Executive Interpretation

The organisation is at the beginning of its post-quantum readiness journey.

---

## Level 2 — Developing

Characteristics

- Initial governance activities
- Partial technology visibility
- Early planning
- Limited migration preparation

Executive Interpretation

The organisation has recognised the challenge but requires structured planning.

---

## Level 3 — Managed

Characteristics

- Governance established
- Technology documented
- Cryptographic inventory improving
- Formal planning underway

Executive Interpretation

The organisation has established the foundations required for enterprise transformation.

---

## Level 4 — Optimised

Characteristics

- Governance embedded
- Enterprise-wide visibility
- Migration programme active
- Continuous assessment

Executive Interpretation

The organisation is actively preparing for post-quantum migration.

---

## Level 5 — Quantum Ready

Characteristics

- Mature governance
- Enterprise cryptographic agility
- Continuous monitoring
- Executive oversight
- Strategic readiness

Executive Interpretation

The organisation demonstrates enterprise-level readiness for post-quantum transformation.

---

# Assessment Method

Each assessment domain contributes evidence towards the overall maturity evaluation.

The framework combines organisational context, technology posture, cryptographic posture and governance capability to determine the Transformation Readiness level.

Future versions of EQRAF will introduce weighted domain evaluation and adaptive scoring algorithms.

---

# Executive Reporting

The maturity model supports executive reporting by translating technical findings into business-focused capability statements.

Rather than asking "How many vulnerabilities exist?", the framework answers questions such as:

- How prepared is the organisation?
- What capabilities are missing?
- What should be prioritised?
- What actions should executives take next?
